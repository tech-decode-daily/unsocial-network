# Unsocial Network privacy notice

The other guy publishes Unsocial Network. This notice covers the Windows desktop app, version 0.1.3. Updated November 25, 2026.

## When you join a room

You choose a display name and connect to the computer or server hosting the room. The host processes your network address, room membership, connection status and the times of room activity to connect participants and deliver messages and files. Other room members can see your display name and presence. Share an invitation privately because anyone with a valid invitation can join.

Messages, file contents, filenames and file metadata that you choose to share go to the room host and are available to room members. If you host a room website, members can request supported assets from the folder you select. The app encrypts the connection to the host using TLS. The host can read room content. The publisher receives room content only if you connect to a room operated by the publisher or separately send content to the publisher.

## Storage and your choices

Desktop-hosted rooms expire after 24 hours by default. The host cleans up expired room records and shared files. Standalone server operators can configure room lifetimes. Participants can keep downloaded files, and hosts may retain backups. Leaving a room revokes your membership. Previously shared content can remain available until the room expires.

Your computer stores preferences and downloaded files. The app uses operating-system protected storage for saved memberships, local transfer paths and queue information when available. You choose which files to send, can stop sharing your room website, and can delete downloaded files from your device. Contact the room operator about data held on that host.

## Publisher contact

The desktop app has no built-in advertising or usage analytics. For questions about this notice, contact the publisher through the [Unsocial Network support page](https://github.com/tech-decode-daily/unsocial-network/issues). Support issues are public. Keep room invitations, private messages, files and other personal information out of public issues.
